4. Identify 3 LIKELY OBJECTIONS and write a response for each:

   - Consider their current creative strategy, budget level, and brand positioning
   - Common objection categories: cost concerns, brand fit worries, already have an agency/in-house team, unproven format, timing/bandwidth, need to see results first
   - Pick the 3 most likely for THIS brand specifically, not generic ones
   - For each objection, write a calm, direct response. No begging. No overselling. Just truth and proof.
   - Reference the Stradvert case study where relevant: street interview ads for an AI trading platform hit £0.86 cost per purchase vs £1.54-£2.48 UGC benchmark (44-65% cheaper)
   - Tone: you've heard this before, you're not rattled, you have an answer

5. Recommend a PACKAGE based on what you've learned:
   - Starter (£3K/mo) - for brands testing the format, lower ad spend, early stage
   - Growth (£5K/mo) - for brands with proven product-market fit ready to scale creative
   - Scale (£10K/mo) - for brands spending £20K+ on ads monthly, need creative volume
   - Elite (£20K/mo) - for brands spending £50K+ monthly, full creative partnership
   - Give a clear recommendation with a one-line reason why
   - If there's a case for upselling to the next tier, note it as a stretch goal

6. Write 5 TALKING POINTS for the call:
   - Each one should be specific to this brand, not generic
   - Reference something real about their ads, creative, competitors, or market position
   - These are things Ryan would say on the call to show he's done his homework
   - Format: the point itself, then a one-line note on why it lands
   - The talking points should build a narrative: establish credibility, surface the gap in their current approach, position street interviews as the answer, handle the "why now", and close with next steps
   - No buzzwords. No fluff. Talk like a person who knows their stuff.

   - Write like you're briefing yourself before a call, not presenting to a board
   - Confident and direct. You know the format works. You're not hoping for permission.
   - No corporate language. No em dashes. No bullet-point buzzword lists.
   - Be specific. "Their UGC feels templated" beats "their creative could be improved"
   - Swear if it fits. Be blunt. These notes are for your eyes only.
   - The energy is "I've seen their ads, I know what's wrong, and I know we can fix it"
   - Never sound desperate or salesy. You're choosing whether THEY are a good fit too.
